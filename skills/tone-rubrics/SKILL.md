# Persona & Tone Adaptation Rubrics

This skill defines the stylistic rules, syntax choices, and vocabulary registers for calibrating humanized writing across distinct target personas.

---

## 1. Casual / Conversational Tone

- **Audience:** Blog readers, social audiences, community discussions, newsletter subscribers.
- **Voice:** Direct, friendly, approachable, and authentic. Feels like a conversation with an experienced colleague over coffee.
- **Syntactic Rules:**
  - Mandatory natural contractions (*don't, can't, it's, we've, you'll*).
  - First and second person are encouraged (*"I tested this...", "You probably noticed..."*).
  - High burstiness: frequent punchy fragments (*"Not quite.", "Fair enough."*).
  - Informal coordinating transitions (*"And that's okay.", "So what gives?"*).
  - Occasional conversational idioms and dry humor where appropriate.

---

## 2. Professional / Executive Tone

- **Audience:** Business leaders, stakeholders, board members, management teams.
- **Voice:** Confident, decisive, clear, and bottom-line focused. Cuts corporate jargon and vague platitudes.
- **Syntactic Rules:**
  - Moderate contraction usage (*it's, doesn't* when natural, avoiding stiffness).
  - Active voice: put actors before actions (*"Management approved the budget", not "The budget was approved"*).
  - Front-load conclusions: state business impacts and quantifiable metrics early.
  - Ban corporate buzzwords (*"synergize", "circle back", "paradigm shift", "holistic solution"*).
  - Short, authoritative statements for strategic takeaways.

---

## 3. Academic / Scholarly Tone

- **Audience:** Researchers, peer reviewers, academic readers, students.
- **Voice:** Rigorous, precise, evidence-grounded, and intellectually honest.
- **Syntactic Rules:**
  - Minimal contractions; formal register.
  - Eliminate pseudo-academic AI filler (*"It is important to acknowledge that this study highlights..."* -> *"This study demonstrates..."*).
  - Nuanced qualification without evasive hedging: state limitations clearly with supporting evidence.
  - Sophisticated multi-clause syntax balanced with clear topic sentences to maintain readability.
  - Accurate technical terminology used with exact precision rather than decorative flourish.

---

## 4. Storyteller / Narrative Tone

- **Audience:** Essay readers, creative nonfiction consumers, long-form narrative fans.
- **Voice:** Immersive, sensory, emotionally grounded, and evocative.
- **Syntactic Rules:**
  - High dynamic range in sentence lengths: quiet contemplative descriptions punctuated by rapid-fire narrative beats.
  - Strong, vivid physical verbs replacing weak "was/were + adjective" constructions.
  - Show, don't tell: ground abstract ideas in concrete, physical details.
  - Thoughtful cadence with deliberate rhythm pauses (em-dashes, ellipses where earned).
